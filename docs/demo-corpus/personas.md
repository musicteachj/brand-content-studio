# Verdure Materials — Audience Personas

Verdure's LinkedIn audience is professional and technical. Content should speak to these three. Knowing the reader shapes which evidence to lead with and how much to explain.

## Persona 1 — "Priya, the Sustainability-Led Architect"

- **Role:** Associate at a mid-size practice; specifies materials and owns the carbon story on bids.
- **Goals:** Hit embodied-carbon targets without blowing the budget or the program; win work by being genuinely (not performatively) low-carbon.
- **Pains:** Suppliers who can't produce real LCA data; "sustainable" products that fall apart under scrutiny at value engineering; being asked to greenwash by clients or PMs.
- **What wins her:** Hard numbers, published LCA boundaries, honesty about trade-offs, getting Verdure into the room at RIBA Stage 2.
- **How to write for her:** Lead with the metric and the project example. Respect that she already cares — give her ammunition, not a sermon.

## Persona 2 — "Marcus, the Pragmatic Developer / Client"

- **Role:** Development manager balancing returns, risk, and an ESG commitment he has to actually report.
- **Goals:** A defensible low-carbon story for investors and planning, delivered on time and on budget.
- **Pains:** Cost premiums he can't justify; program risk from unfamiliar materials; ESG claims that won't survive due diligence.
- **What wins him:** Cost modeled honestly (both ways), de-risking via case studies and warranties, the marketing/asset-value upside of a credibly green building.
- **How to write for him:** Acknowledge cost head-on. Translate carbon into business terms — asset value, planning advantage, reportable ESG. Never pretend green is always cheaper.

## Persona 3 — "Dan, the Skeptical Contractor / Specifier"

- **Role:** Technical lead or main contractor who has to build the thing and stand behind it.
- **Goals:** Materials that install predictably, perform long-term, and won't generate callbacks.
- **Pains:** Bio-based materials he suspects are fragile or fussy; cure times and sequencing that wreck the program; "eco" products with thin technical support.
- **What wins him:** Durability and service-life data, clear installation guidance, candor about cure time and constraints, responsive technical support.
- **How to write for him:** Lead with performance and longevity, not values. Answer "will it last and will it slow me down?" before anything else. Plain language, no preaching.

## Cross-cutting note

All three are time-poor and read on mobile between meetings. None of them respond to guilt, hype, or vague green adjectives. Evidence and candor are the universal currency.
