Hempcrete sequesters roughly 110 kg of CO₂ per cubic meter. That's not an offset we bought — it's carbon locked into the wall for the life of the building.

We mention this a lot, so here's the honest caveat: hempcrete isn't structural. It's an insulating infill, paired with a timber or steel frame. Cure time is longer than block. The cost premium runs 5–12% on the envelope.

When is it worth it? When you're chasing a low embodied-carbon target and you value breathable walls that buffer humidity for decades. On the Larkfield block, it took the envelope from a liability to the best-performing line in the LCA.

Specify it for what it is, not what you wish it were.

A reclaimed oak beam has already paid its carbon debt. The tree grew, the carbon was captured, the milling happened — decades ago.

When we reclaim and re-mill it for cladding, the cradle-to-gate footprint is a fraction of new timber, and a rounding error next to aluminum or uPVC.

The catch is supply. Reclaimed stock is finite and inconsistent — you spec to availability, not the other way around. We'd rather tell you that upfront than oversell a hero material we can't deliver at volume.

#ReclaimedTimber #CircularEconomy #EmbodiedCarbon

"Sustainable" without a number is just a feeling.

We ask every spec to attach the metric: embodied carbon in kgCO₂e/m², thermal performance in U-value, end-of-life pathway. A material that can't show its numbers doesn't belong in a low-carbon building.

This isn't pedantry. It's the difference between a project that hits its target and one that finds out at handover that it didn't.

Mycelium grows into the exact shape of its mold in about a week, then we heat-set it to stop growth. The result: an acoustic panel that's home-compostable at end of life.

We installed 400 of them in the Halden studio fit-out. Reverberation time dropped from 1.4s to 0.6s. The architect's note back to us: "first acoustic product I haven't had to hide."

Bio-based doesn't have to mean visually apologetic. It can be the thing you put on show.

The greenest material is often the one you didn't replace.

Before we quote a single panel, we ask whether the existing fabric can be retained. We've talked clients out of full re-clads more than once. It costs us a sale and earns us the next three.

Reuse first, then bio-based new build, then recycled, then virgin as a last resort. That hierarchy is older than our company and we didn't invent it — we just refuse to skip steps.

Embodied carbon is the carbon spent before anyone turns on a light — extraction, manufacture, transport, construction. For a new building it can equal 20+ years of operational emissions, paid up front, the day you open.

Operational carbon gets the headlines because it's easy to meter. Embodied carbon is harder to see and just as real.

If your decarbonization plan stops at the energy bill, it stops halfway.

#EmbodiedCarbon #WholeLifeCarbon #NetZero

A question we get from contractors: "Will this bio-based stuff actually last?"

Fair. Here's the data we lead with. Our hempcrete walls have a documented service life of 50–100 years and improve in compressive strength as they carbonate. Reclaimed timber that's already stood for a century has proven it. Mycelium acoustic panels are warrantied for 15 years indoors.

Longevity is a sustainability metric. A material that fails early and goes to landfill was never green, whatever the brochure said.

Breathability is the unglamorous superpower of bio-based walls.

Hempcrete and lime let moisture move through the wall assembly instead of trapping it. The payoff: no interstitial condensation, healthier indoor air, and a fabric that doesn't rot from the inside the way some sealed high-performance assemblies can.

Airtight is good. Airtight without a moisture strategy is a maintenance bill waiting to arrive.

#Breathability #BuildingScience #Hempcrete

Cradle-to-gate vs. cradle-to-grave — the boundary you choose changes the whole story.

A material can look great cradle-to-gate (low manufacturing carbon) and terrible cradle-to-grave (lands in a hole at end of life). We publish both boundaries for every product because picking the flattering one is how the industry got comfortable lying to itself.

Ask any supplier — including us — where their numbers stop.

Cost is the objection we respect most, so let's be straight about it.

Bio-based materials often carry a 5–15% premium at the envelope. Sometimes that premium disappears once you account for thermal mass cutting plant size, or for the marketing value of a genuinely low-carbon building. Sometimes it doesn't, and the right answer is a hybrid spec.

We'll model it both ways and show you the honest number. A supplier who pretends green is always cheaper is a supplier who'll let you down at value engineering.
