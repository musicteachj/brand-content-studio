# Verdure Materials — Voice & Tone Guide

## Who we are

Verdure Materials supplies low-carbon, bio-based building materials — mycelium acoustic panels, hempcrete blocks, and reclaimed-timber cladding — to architects, specifiers, and developers who treat sustainability as a structural decision, not a marketing layer. We sell to people who read the technical data sheet before the brochure.

## Brand personality

We are the **quietly expert specialist** in the room. Think of a materials scientist who also happens to care deeply about the planet and can explain embodied carbon to a skeptical contractor without condescension. Verdure is:

- **Grounded** — we talk in numbers, certifications, and tested performance, never vibes.
- **Optimistic but unsentimental** — we believe the built environment can decarbonize, and we prove it with data rather than guilt.
- **Craft-respecting** — we honor the trades and the architects who specify us; we never talk down to the people who build.
- **Plain-spoken** — we'd rather be clear than clever. We explain jargon the first time we use it.

## Voice principles

1. **Lead with the number, then the meaning.** "Hempcrete sequesters ~110 kg of CO₂ per cubic meter — roughly what a mature tree absorbs in a decade." Always anchor a claim to evidence.
2. **Respect the reader's intelligence.** Our audience are professionals. No hand-holding, no greenwashing, no "save the planet" platitudes.
3. **Concrete over abstract.** Name the project, the material, the result. "On the Larkfield mixed-use block we cut embodied carbon 38%" beats "our materials reduce carbon."
4. **Honesty about trade-offs.** If a material has a longer cure time or a cost premium, we say so and explain when it's worth it. Credibility is our moat.
5. **The planet is the point, not the pitch.** We don't perform sustainability. We assume the reader already cares and give them the tools to act.

## Tone by context

- **LinkedIn posts:** confident, specific, lightly warm. One idea per post. Open with a hook that's a fact or a tension, not a question. Close with a quiet call to think or act, never a hard sell.
- **Technical data:** precise, neutral, exhaustive. Units always stated. Sources cited.
- **Case studies:** narrative but evidence-led — challenge, material choice, measured outcome.

## Words we use

embodied carbon · life-cycle assessment (LCA) · sequestration · bio-based · circularity · specification · regenerative · cradle-to-gate · thermal mass · breathability · reclaimed · mycelium · hempcrete

## Words we avoid

eco-friendly · green (as a vague adjective) · save the planet · guilt-free · all-natural · game-changer · revolutionary · sustainable (without a number attached) · "do your part" · any exclamation-point urgency

## Formatting habits

- Short paragraphs, generous whitespace — these posts are read on phones between meetings.
- Numbers as digits ("38%", "110 kg"), units always written out the first time.
- Em dashes for asides; no emoji in technical posts (an occasional restrained 🌱 is acceptable only in lighter culture posts, never in data posts).
- Hashtags: 3–5 max, professional (#EmbodiedCarbon #SustainableConstruction #BioBasedMaterials), never #blessed-style filler.
